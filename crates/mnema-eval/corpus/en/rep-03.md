# Vendor assessment: Sablewire message queue

Northgate has rented a managed message queue from Sablewire since 2023, and the contract comes up for renewal in November. This assessment exists to answer one question: renew, renegotiate, or leave. It draws on the billing record, two support transcripts, and the August incident review.

Sablewire scores seven out of ten overall, which is enough to renew but not enough to expand. That number is a weighted average of four categories, and it hides a wide spread: two categories are strong, one is adequate, and one is poor.

Reliability is the poor category. The published availability commitment is ninety-nine point nine percent over a calendar month, and August finished below it. Sablewire acknowledged the breach without argument and issued a credit of two thousand four hundred euros, which is the contractual amount and does not begin to cover what the day cost us.

Support is adequate rather than good. Our August page was answered in fifty-two minutes against a promised thirty, and the first two replies were plainly templated. Once a named engineer picked the case up the quality changed completely, and the eventual root-cause note was better than anything we could have written ourselves.

Price is the strongest category by a distance. The queue costs thirty-one thousand euros a year, roughly a third of what the two obvious alternatives quoted, and the price has not moved across two renewals. Moving away would cost more in engineering time than three years of that difference.

Documentation is the fourth category and the second strong one. The reference material is accurate, versioned alongside the product, and honest about what the queue does not guarantee, which is rarer than it should be. Two of our engineers learned the failure modes from that material rather than from us, and both said afterwards that it saved them a week.

The recommendation is to renew for twelve months rather than the twenty-four Sablewire offered, and to keep the migration work funded in parallel. A short term costs about four percent more per month and buys the option to leave without a fight. Neither this assessment nor the incident review argues for an immediate exit.
